What you are seeing

Positions are real. Planets follow JPL's Keplerian elements; dwarf planets and comets use osculating elements from the JPL Small-Body Database. Checked against JPL Horizons, the planets land within about 0.15° of their true positions between 1800 and 2050 (outside that window they drift, so the date shows "≈ approx."). The Moon follows Meeus's lunar theory, within about 0.005° of JPL, so phases and eclipses land at the right time and place.

Orientation is real. Each world spins about its true pole at its true rate. Earth's day and night match the clock, Saturn's rings tilt toward us exactly as they do in the sky, and Uranus rolls on its side.

Schematic view squeezes distances (square-root scale) and enlarges the worlds so everything fits. True scale shows real distances and real sizes: the planets shrink to specks, which is why the markers are there. Moons other than ours use real periods and distances with illustrative starting positions. Shadows (eclipses, moon transits, ring shadows) are computed from true geometry.

Earth imagery: Solar System Scope (CC BY 4.0) via three.js; Moon texture from the three.js examples. All other surfaces, the Sun and the Milky Way are generated in shaders. Built with three.js.
